## Title

**Crystallography from a lift condition**

## Abstract

*Finite point groups from a Heisenberg lift: a Lorentzian daughter metric, its positivity compactifier, and an integrality lattice generate crystallographic classes as a function of the observer, without a hand-chosen direction.*

Starting from a single deep wedge in the orthogonal Lie algebra so(5,3), the induced metric on the wedge's
transverse quotient has signature exactly (3,1), and the equivariance of the
degree-0 floor built over it *forces* that same metric as the unique so(W)-equivariant bracket of family I
. On this structure two compactifiers are measured — a
positivity compactifier cutting the Levi to a compact SO(3)×SO(2) and
an arithmetic thickness of the integrality stabiliser Γ (measured as a thick/thin *contrast*; on the split
stratum the covolume of the arithmetic group O⁺(I_{3,1}) = [3,4,4] is *computed*, V = 0.0763304662,
matching the orbit density 1/(48V) = 0.27294 against a direct count 0.2727 — the general finite-covolume
theorem remaining 📖 Borel–Harish-Chandra) — whose weave gives a finite-volume moduli. A canonical lattice does *not* exist: the daughter's
symmetry group acts with 3-dimensional orbits on an irreducible W, and finite
states never yield one. What does exist is a *class* of lattices (the lift condition), and — shown on the standard ℤ⁴⊗ℤ² — finite
point groups arise as (compact stabiliser) ∩ (discrete arithmetic) per stratum, so the crystallographic
class is a *pair* (Λ, t) — the same integral Lorentzian lattice I_{3,1}, read over different timelike
sections t^⊥, realises all seven 3D holohedries (cubic, tetragonal, hexagonal, orthorhombic, monoclinic,
trigonal (rhombohedral), triclinic), each identified by composition. Which holohedry a section carries is a
*classification*, not a representative-dependent lower bound: for p ≢ 1 (mod 8), a rank-p section of I_{p,1} exists iff its
discriminant form is cyclic ⟨1/n⟩ (a quadratic-residue condition) — the law's forward leg derived on every
section, its reverse derived for p ≢ 1 (mod 8); at p ≡ 1 (mod 8) the reverse leg *fails*, since the
discriminant form does not fix the parity of the ambient. Read as a function of the parent, the map singles out the signature with exactly one timelike direction — q_d = 1, as geometry — by a
qualitative break (a definite section exists iff q_d = 1), the unique signature with one observer to one
finite crystal; the dimension d itself is *not* singled out and remains an input. An author pre-registration that this variety would degenerate with dimension is a measured miss — over the
swept range the validated holohedry-image count [3, 7, ≥13] grows (a bounded result, not an asymptotic
refutation). **No new constant is introduced; the single dimensional input is
the ambient Λ-scale inherited by the programme.** **No physical reading of any object
— "time", "energy", "mass" — is made or implied; where a compactness or covolume statement is classical it
is marked 📖, not measured.**

## §3. Why there is no canonical lattice
No canonical lattice exists on the daughter space W, and this is one fact together with the metric-forcing daughter result carried by the same evidence, not two separate claims: the so(η|W)-orbits are three-dimensional everywhere it was checked — on timelike, spacelike, and lightlike vectors, and on 200 random probes, with orbit dimension measured minimum 3 and maximum 3 — and W is irreducible under so(η|W) (dim End(W) = 1, zero invariant lines); consequently any so(η|W)-invariant set containing a nonzero vector must contain a three-dimensional manifold and cannot be discrete. The scope of this result is the canonical, i.e. fully invariant, lattice: it says nothing about a lattice realized as a symmetry-breaking state rather than an invariant one, and the minimality of its three counted inputs — scale, a splitting choice, and the lattice datum itself — taken together as a set has not been established and stands as an open address. This is a measured absence, not an unfinished search: the mechanism is the collision of W's irreducibility with a three-dimensional orbit floor holding on every causal type probed, so the negative is a structural property of the invariant floor rather than a gap in coverage.

Passing to symmetry-breaking states reopens the question without adding a new input. ## §6a. Independence of the two conditions, and calibration on known cases

The finite point groups of §6 arise as an *intersection* — a compact positivity stabiliser with a
discrete arithmetic group — and that phrasing earns its weight only if both conditions are necessary.
A single detector, {U ∈ PointGroup(Λ): UJ = JU}, applied to three worlds shows they are: dropping
either condition breaks "finite and nontrivial". With a β-integral lattice but an *indefinite* J′ the commuting set is
infinite — 204 commuting elements, among them P ⊗ I of infinite order — so positivity is necessary;
with a positive J₀ but a *generic* (non-lifting) lattice only ±I of 1188 candidates survive (effective
order 1, trivial) — so integrality is necessary; and with both the group is finite and nontrivial
(192, effective 96, element orders [1,2,3,4,6,12] as in §6). The three worlds are named cases on ℤ⁴ ⊗ ℤ² and a generic M·ℤ⁸, not a sweep
of all combinations, and the value of the trichotomy is that one code runs all three — it repackages
two already-established facts (the finite 192 and the infinite hyperbolic order) into a single detector,
rather than adding a new measurement.

Two known cases calibrate the construction, and the two carry *different* epistemic weight — a
distinction drawn explicitly. The automorphism datum is a genuine blind reproduction: the A-module of §6
was computed by integer enumeration of K ∩ Γ — a scan of the source for closed-form CM tokens is empty
— and it returns ℤ/4, ℤ/6, ℤ/2 at τ = i, ρ, generic, which are exactly the automorphism groups
Aut(E_τ) of the corresponding elliptic curves (📖, Silverman, *Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves* III.10);
neither source saw the other, so this is a reproduced known result. The polarisation datum is weaker and is stated as a *calibration*, not a
reproduction: the lift form was *built* from prescribed elementary divisors, so recovering those
divisors in pairs (d, d) is arithmetic of the Frobenius normal form, not an independent recovery of
Mumford's theorem — our lift datum (c, Smith(B/c)) coincides with Mumford's polarisation type, and the
non-tautological content is only the identification of the two and the distinction of principal from
non-principal ((1,1) versus (1,4)).
The finite group orders on the *adapted* split strata — 192, 288, 96, matched by the full
order-histogram — are closed by Lemma 6: each factors as the tabulated cubic holohedry (order 48) times
Aut(E_τ) ∈ {ℤ/4, ℤ/6, ℤ/2} at τ = i, ρ, generic, none new, with
Brown–Bülow–Neubüser–Wondratschek–Zassenhaus (1978, 📖) as the ceiling reference. The two dim-2 strata

## §9. The formal spine — definitions, lemmas, and the Main Theorem (Theorem 1)

This section states formally what §§1–8 established, so the claim reads as a theorem with hypotheses. Each item carries its status: **derived** (a proof is given here, a few lines, needing no probe), **measured** (it rests on a probe), **📖** (an imported theorem, multiplicity 0). Nothing is read physically. Component groups are named explicitly throughout — O, SO, O⁺, SO⁺ — and the central quotient factor ± is kept beside each group. Statements of this paper are cited across the series by their global identifiers P6.Def k, P6.Lem k, P6.Thm k.

### Definitions

**Definition 0 (the data).** Let (W, η) be the daughter: a real quadratic space of signature Σ = (p_d, q_d), d := p_d + q_d. Let (A, ω) = (ℝ², ω) be the sl(2)-module with its symplectic form. Put V := W ⊗ A and **B := η ⊗ ω**, an alternating nondegenerate form on V. The Heisenberg algebra is n := V ⊕ ℝX with [v, v′] := B(v, v′)·X; H := exp n is the simply-connected group (dim 2d+1), and Z := exp ℝX its one-dimensional centre. The symmetry group is **G := O(η) × SL(A)**, acting on V as g ⊗ h; it preserves B, and the action map G → Sp(V, B) has kernel ⟨(−1, −1)⟩, so its image is G/⟨(−1, −1)⟩ ⊂ Sp(V, B) (this ± is carried wherever G acts on V). Write 𝒥 := {J ∈ End V : J² = −1, B(J·, J·) = B, B(x, Jx) > 0 for x ≠ 0} for the Siegel space of compatible positive complex structures, and K_J := Stab_G(J) ⊂ G. Since the action G → Sp(V, B) has kernel ⟨(−1,−1)⟩, the stabiliser subgroups live in G *without* a quotient; we write K̄_J for the image of K_J in Sp(V, B) — a quotient of K_J by the ⟨(−1,−1)⟩ it contains — and all `/⟨(−1,−1)⟩` equalities below are stated for the barred (image) groups.

**Definition 1 (β-integrality — the exact lift datum).** A lattice Λ ⊂ V (rank 2d) is *β-integral* if B(Λ, Λ) ⊂ cℤ for some c > 0; let c(Λ) be the positive generator of B(Λ, Λ) ⊂ ℝ. Its *type* is the tuple of Smith invariant factors (d₁ | … | d_d) of B/c(Λ) on Λ — the polarisation type (📖 Mumford 1966).

**Definition 2 (adapted J; observer; section — the classified stratum).** For a negative-definite q_d-plane N ⊂ W and τ ∈ ℍ (the upper half-plane), set S_N := (+1 on N^{⊥η}, −1 on N), so S_N ∈ O(η) with S_N² = 1, and **J_{N,τ} := S_N ⊗ J_τ**. An *observer* is a point N ∈ Gr⁻_{q_d}(W); for q_d = 1 it is a timelike line ℝt. Relative to a lattice Λ_W ⊂ W it is *rational* when N is spanned by lattice vectors; the *section* is **S(Λ_W, N) := Λ_W ∩ N^{⊥η}**, a positive-definite lattice of rank p_d, with *holohedry* the full orthogonal group O(S(Λ_W, N)). **The section classification (Theorem 1 below) is stated on the G-rational, split stratum Λ = Λ_W ⊗ Λ_A only; the generic and non-split strata are not covered by it.**

**Definition 3 (G-rationality).** A lattice Λ ⊂ V is *G-rational* if G is ℚ-defined relative to Λ — i.e. Λ carries a ℚ-structure with respect to which G is defined over ℚ. A *split* Λ = Λ_W ⊗ Λ_A (with η(Λ_W, Λ_W) ⊂ ℚ) is one example, not the definition: non-split ℚ-forms of G (through restriction of scalars) also occur. Put Γ_Λ := Stab_G(Λ) ⊂ G (without a quotient — for a split Λ, Γ_Λ = O(Λ_W) × SL(Λ_A)), and write Γ̄_Λ for its image in Sp(V, B).

### Lemmas

**Lemma 1 (the metric is forced — derived; blind to signature).** On the degree-0 floor the daughter metric is not merely admitted by so(η)-equivariance but forced, and this holds for any nondegenerate η in any signature.

*Proof.* Decompose Λ²V = (Λ²W ⊗ S²A) ⊕ (S²W ⊗ Λ²A). Now so(η) acts trivially on A, so the so(η)-invariants of the target Λ²A are all of Λ²A = ℝ·ω. In the source, for d ≥ 3 the standard representation W of so(η) is *absolutely irreducible*, so End_{so(η)}(W) = ℝ and the invariants of W ⊗ W ≅ End(W) are one-dimensional, spanned by η ∈ S²W; this single word fixes both counts — (S²W)^{so(η)} = ℝ·η and (Λ²W)^{so(η)} = 0. Hence Hom_{so(η)}(Λ²V, Λ²A) = Hom_{so(η)}(S²W ⊗ Λ²A, Λ²A) = ℝ·(η ⊗ ω), one-dimensional, and the unique so(η)-equivariant bracket of family I is B = η ⊗ ω up to scale. ∎

The dimension count does not see the signature of η: nothing in the proof distinguishes (3,1) from (2,2) or (4,0), so the singling-out of (3,1) happens in the section geometry of Definition 2, not here. The numerical measurement dim Hom = 1 at residual 10⁻¹⁶ is the shadow of this lemma; the bracket's −1 normalisation is fixed in the named wedge convention.

**Lemma 2 (no invariant discrete subgroup — derived).** For d ≥ 2, V = W ⊗ A admits no nonzero SO⁺(η)-invariant discrete subgroup; in particular no invariant lattice. A discrete translation group can appear only as a symmetry-broken state.

*Proof.* A connected group fixing a discrete set fixes it pointwise, and fixing by the group SO⁺(η) is annihilation by its Lie algebra so(η). So a nonzero SO⁺(η)-invariant discrete subgroup would contain a nonzero so(η)-fixed vector v ∈ V = W ⊗ A ≅ W^{⊕2} (A trivial under so(η)), hence a nonzero so(η)-fixed vector in W. But W has no trivial so(η)-subsummand: for d ≥ 3 it is absolutely irreducible (Lemma 1); for d = 2 the single generator of so(η) — so(2) at Σ = (2,0)/(0,2), so(1,1) at Σ = (1,1) — acts on W with nonzero eigenvalues ±1 (a rotation weight, or a boost eigenvalue) and so has no nonzero kernel, the case Σ = (1,1) included. Either way (W)^{so(η)} = 0 — a contradiction. ∎

The measured shadow: the so(η)-orbit dimension is exactly 3 on every causal type probed and across 200 random probes, and W is irreducible (dim End(W) = 1), so an invariant set with a nonzero vector cannot be discrete. This is a structural absence, not an unfinished search — the collision of W's irreducibility with a three-dimensional orbit floor.

**Lemma 3 (the lift is β-integrality — derived).** With c(Λ) the positive generator of B(Λ, Λ) (Definition 1), a lattice Λ ⊂ V generates a discrete subgroup (a lattice) H_Λ := ⟨exp Λ⟩ of the Heisenberg group H if and only if Λ is β-integral; then H_Λ ∩ Z = exp(½·c(Λ)·ℤ·X), and c(tΛ) = t²·c(Λ).

*Proof.* H is 2-step nilpotent, so BCH truncates: exp u · exp v = exp(u + v + ½B(u, v)·X). For u, v ∈ Λ we have −u−v ∈ Λ, and exp u · exp v · exp(−u−v) = exp(½B(u, v)·X) ∈ H_Λ; ranging over Λ, the words with zero V-part fill exactly exp(½·B(Λ, Λ)·X) = exp(½·c(Λ)·ℤ·X), so H_Λ ∩ Z = exp(½·c(Λ)·ℤ·X) and H_Λ/(H_Λ ∩ Z) ≅ Λ. The subgroup B(Λ, Λ) ⊂ ℝ is finitely generated; it is discrete ⟺ cyclic ⟺ Λ is β-integral, and otherwise dense in ℝ, whereupon H_Λ ∩ Z is dense and H_Λ is not discrete. So H_Λ is discrete ⟺ Λ is β-integral, and then it is a lattice in H. Finally B(tv, tv′) = t²B(v, v′) gives c(tΛ) = t²·c(Λ). ∎

The measured shadow: of 50 generic lattices tested, 0 pass the integrality condition MᵀBM integral up to one common factor, and c(tΛ) = t²·c(Λ) holds exactly, matching the grading element δ. Malcev's theorem (📖 Malcev 1949, multiplicity 0) then supplies the lattice in H, its rational-structure-constants hypothesis met in the basis Λ ∪ {c(Λ)X}.

**Lemma 4ab (positivity gives a compact stabiliser — derived).** Write K̄_J for the image of K_J = Stab_G(J) in Sp(V, B). (a) For every J ∈ 𝒥, K̄_J is compact — and so is K_J, a finite ⟨(−1,−1)⟩-extension of it — for every J. (b) The adapted J_{N,τ} := S_N ⊗ J_τ lies in 𝒥, its image stabiliser is K̄_{J_{N,τ}} = (O(η|N^{⊥η}) × O(η|N) × SO(2)_τ)/⟨(−1, −1)⟩ of dimension p_d(p_d−1)/2 + q_d(q_d−1)/2 + 1, and its orbit is G·J_{N,τ} ≅ G/K = Gr⁻_{q_d}(W) × ℍ².

*Proof.* (a) A J ∈ 𝒥 makes (V, J, B(·, J·)) Hermitian, and the subgroup of GL(V) preserving both J and B is the compact unitary group U(V, J, B) ≅ U(d). The image of G in Sp(V, B) is closed: the image of a homomorphism of linear algebraic groups is a closed subgroup (standard, e.g. Borel, *Linear Algebraic Groups*), and the image of G(ℝ) is an open, finite-index subgroup of the real points of that image, hence closed. So K̄_J = (image of G) ∩ U(V, J, B) is closed in a compact group, hence compact; and K_J, a finite ⟨(−1,−1)⟩-extension of K̄_J, is compact with it. (b) Compatibility: S_N ∈ O(η) and J_τ ∈ Sp(ω) give J_{N,τ}² = −1 with J_{N,τ} preserving B; positivity is the tensor η_N ⊗ ω_τ of two positive-definite forms, η_N := η∘S_N being positive-definite since S_N flips the sign on N. An element (g, h) ∈ G stabilises S_N ⊗ J_τ iff gS_Ng⁻¹ = λS_N and hJ_τh⁻¹ = λ⁻¹J_τ with λ² = 1 (as S_N² = 1). The value λ = −1 is excluded on the A-side: an h with hJ_τh⁻¹ = −J_τ anticommutes with J_τ, so has the form [[a, b], [b, −a]] with det = −(a²+b²) < 0, not in SL(2,ℝ). Hence λ = 1, so g commutes with S_N — preserving both eigenspaces, g ∈ O(η|N^{⊥η}) × O(η|N) — and h ∈ centraliser(J_τ) = SO(2)_τ; modulo ⟨(−1,−1)⟩ this is the stated K̄. The A-side exclusion of λ = −1 holds even at p_d = q_d, where the W-side alone would permit the swap N ↔ N^{⊥η}, so a general Σ is covered. Finally O(η)/(O(η|N^{⊥η}) × O(η|N)) = Gr⁻_{q_d}(W) and SL(A)/SO(2) = ℍ². ∎

For Σ = (3,1) the dimension is 3 + 0 + 1 = 4 with identity component SO(3) × SO(2), and G/K = H³ × ℍ² (the moduli of §5). The measured shadow: the Killing form of the positive stabiliser is negative-definite ([−4..−4], dim 4, SO(3)×SO(2)), while an indefinite J′ keeps a boost ([−4..+4], noncompact). Uniqueness of the maximal-dimension adapted orbit — with the strata dim K_J ∈ {4,2,2,0} as its shadow — is not derived here and stands as an open address.

**Lemma 5 (arithmeticity — derived (a,b); (c) via 📖 Borel–Harish-Chandra; (d) via 📖 Borel density).** Let Λ ⊂ V be G-rational (Definition 3). Then Γ_Λ is arithmetic in G, and the adapted moduli (G/K)/Γ_Λ has finite volume; this holds only on the G-rational stratum.

*Proof.* (a) By Lemma 1 the G-invariant alternating forms on V are one-dimensional, spanned by B; when G is ℚ-defined relative to Λ (Definition 3) that line is ℚ-defined, so B = c·B_ℚ for a rational form B_ℚ, whence B(Λ, Λ) ⊂ c·ℚ and Λ is β-integral up to scale (a split Λ_W ⊗ Λ_A with η(Λ_W, Λ_W) ⊂ ℚ is one instance). (b) For the ℚ-structure of Λ, Γ_Λ = Stab_G(Λ) = G ∩ GL(Λ) = G(ℤ) is arithmetic in G by definition (in the split case G(ℤ) = O(Λ_W) × SL(Λ_A), an example). (c) [📖 Borel–Harish-Chandra 1962] an arithmetic subgroup of a semisimple group has finite covolume, so vol(G/Γ_Λ) < ∞, whence vol((G/K)/Γ_Λ) < ∞; the quotient is noncompact iff η_ℚ is isotropic (Godement's compactness criterion, 📖; for I_{3,1} it is isotropic). (d) The finite-volume conclusion of (c) is moreover *equivalent* to G-rationality. Here G = O(3,1) × SL(2,ℝ) is semisimple with no compact factors, so if Γ_Λ has finite covolume then the Borel density theorem (📖, applied to Γ_Λ ∩ G⁰, of finite index) makes Γ_Λ Zariski-dense in G, forcing G to be ℚ-defined relative to Λ — i.e. Λ is G-rational (Definition 3); the converse is (b)–(c). So finite volume holds *exactly* on the G-rational stratum, a countable, positive-codimension locus in the β-integral family; a generic β-integral Λ — carrying no ℚ-structure — has Γ_Λ that is not a lattice in G (infinite covolume). Whether such a generic Γ_Λ is moreover finite is an open address, whose measured shadow is the generic stabiliser-dimension 0 across 50 configurations. ∎

For the split Λ = I_{3,1} ⊗ U the group Γ_W = O(I_{3,1}), and O⁺(I_{3,1}) is the Coxeter reflection group [3,4,4] whose covolume is 0.0763304662 — no longer only cited from (c) but computed, so the BHC input becomes a number on this stratum. The measured thick/thin contrast of the orbit density sits against exactly this covolume.

**Lemma 6 (the point group is a product — derived (a,b); measured factorisation).** For a split lattice Λ = Λ_W ⊗ Λ_A with Λ_W η-integral: (a) Γ_Λ = O(Λ_W) × SL(Λ_A), with image Γ̄_Λ = (O(Λ_W) × SL(Λ_A))/⟨(−1, −1)⟩ in Sp; (b) for an adapted J = S_t ⊗ J_τ with **primitive** rational timelike t (the line ℝt is the datum, so its generator may be taken primitive without loss of generality), the point group K̄_J ∩ Γ̄_Λ ≅ (O(L)_± × Aut(E_τ))/⟨(−1, −1)⟩, where L = Λ_W ∩ t^{⊥η} (a positive-definite section, Definition 2), O(L)_± = {φ ∈ O(L) : φ̄ ∈ {±1} on disc L}, and Aut(E_τ) = Stab_{SL(Λ_A)}(τ) ∈ {ℤ/4, ℤ/6, ℤ/2}; moreover O(L)_± = O(L) whenever φ(n) ≤ 2 (n = |t·t| ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}); in general [O(L) : O(L)_±] = |image of O(L) in (ℤ/n)^×/{±1}| — e.g. triclinic n = 43 has O(L) = {±1}, so O(L)_± = O(L) even though φ(43) = 42.

*Proof.* (a) If g ⊗ h ∈ GL(Λ) with g ∈ O(η), h ∈ SL(A), then after scaling by λ both λg and λ⁻¹h are integral, with det(λg)²·det(λ⁻¹h)⁴ = 1 forcing both unimodular and λ² = 1; hence Γ_Λ = O(Λ_W) × SL(Λ_A) in G, its image Γ̄_Λ carrying the /⟨(−1,−1)⟩ (Definition 3). (b) An element (g, h) fixes J = S_t ⊗ J_τ iff g ∈ Stab_{O(Λ_W)}(±t) and h ∈ Stab_{SL(Λ_A)}(τ) = Aut(E_τ). The gluing is explicit both ways: since Λ_W is unimodular and t primitive with t·t = −n, the finite-index sublattice L ⊕ ℤt ⊂ Λ_W realises Λ_W as the graph of an anti-isometry γ : disc(L) ≅ disc(ℤt) = ℤ/n. A g preserving the splitting acts as (φ on L, ε on ℤt), and g preserves Λ_W ⟺ its induced maps (φ̄, ε̄) preserve γ, i.e. φ̄ = ε̄ under γ — this gives at once which pairs (φ, ε) extend to Λ_W and the restriction of Stab_{O(Λ_W)}(±t) onto O(L). With ε ∈ {±1} on ℤt the image is O(L)_± = {φ : φ̄ ∈ {±1}}, and if (ℤ/n)^× = {±1} (i.e. φ(n) ≤ 2) then O(L)_± = O(L) automatically; in general O(L)_± = O(L) ⟺ the image of O(L) in (ℤ/n)^×/{±1} is trivial — so n = 43 with O(L) = {±1} qualifies despite φ(43) = 42. Passing to images gives K̄_J ∩ Γ̄_Λ = (O(L)_± × Aut(E_τ))/⟨(−1, −1)⟩. ∎

The measured factorisation: |K̄_J ∩ Γ̄_Λ| = |O(L)_±|·|Aut(E_τ)| reproduces the **three adapted strata** — 192 = 48·4 (cubic ⊗ ℤ/4, τ = i), 288 = 48·6 (cubic ⊗ ℤ/6, τ = ρ), 96 = 48·2 (cubic ⊗ ℤ/2, generic), effective = pair/2 — matched by the full order-histogram, not by |K̄ ∩ Γ̄| alone, and built as a Kronecker group P ⊂ GL(6,ℤ) acting on L ⊗ Λ_A (the timelike line ℝt is not tracked, so the sign ε is read off separately). The two dim-2 strata (orders 32 and 4) are τ = i and **non-adapted** (dim K_J = 2, and the W-factor is not a rank-3 section holohedry — by the order-4 histogram, 32 = D₄ ⊗ ℤ/4, not tetragonal ⊗ ℤ/2, which would give 8 not 20 elements of order 4), so they lie outside the lemma. On the adapted split stratum this closes the tabulation question: every order is (a tabulated 3D holohedry) × Aut(E_τ), none new.

**Lemma 7 (the reverse direction — derived for p ≢ 1 mod 8; 📖 only at p ≡ 1).** Let L be a positive-definite lattice of rank p whose discriminant group is cyclic, disc(L) ≅ ℤ/n, with discriminant bilinear form ⟨1/n⟩ — equivalently, admitting an anti-isometry γ : disc(L) ≅ disc(⟨−n⟩) (the quadratic-residue condition). If p ≢ 1 (mod 8) then L is a primitive timelike section of I_{p,1}.

*Proof.* The graph of γ in disc(L) ⊕ disc(⟨−n⟩) is an isotropic subgroup, and the corresponding overlattice M with L ⊕ ⟨−n⟩ ⊆ M ⊆ (L ⊕ ⟨−n⟩)^* is unimodular — a direct check: |graph γ| = n and disc(L ⊕ ⟨−n⟩) = n², so disc(M) = n²/n² = 1, no import needed. M has signature (p, 1), and L = (ℤt)^{⊥} is primitive in it (an element (x, y) ∈ M with y = 0 has ȳ = 0, so x̄ = 0 by injectivity of γ, whence x ∈ L; symmetrically ℤt = M ∩ ℚt). An even indefinite unimodular lattice has signature ≡ 0 (mod 8); here sig(M) = p − 1, so if p − 1 ≢ 0 (mod 8) — i.e. p ≢ 1 (mod 8) — M cannot be even, hence is odd. By the classification of indefinite *odd* unimodular lattices (📖 Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, ch. V; Milnor–Husemoller 1973 — the one import), the odd indefinite unimodular lattice of signature (p, 1) is unique, M ≅ I_{p,1} = ⟨1⟩^p ⊕ ⟨−1⟩. Hence L is a primitive timelike section of I_{p,1}. ∎

For the measured signatures p = 2, 3, 4, 5 (p − 1 = 1, 2, 3, 4, none ≡ 0 mod 8) the reverse direction is therefore *derived*, not imported. At p ≡ 1 (mod 8), i.e. p ∈ {1, 9, 17, …}, M could be even and parity alone does not force M ≅ I_{p,1} — and there the reverse direction **fails**, not merely stays imported: E₈ ⊕ ⟨2⟩ (rank 9) has disc ≅ ℤ/2 with b̄ = ½ and so satisfies the condition, yet it is a section only of the even II_{9,1} and not of I_{9,1}, while I₈ ⊕ ⟨2⟩ carries the same discriminant form and *is* a section of I_{9,1}. The discriminant form alone does not fix the parity of the ambient.

### Main Theorem (Theorem 1)

**Main Theorem (Theorem 1) — the classification law.** Let (W, η) be the daughter of ℝ^{5,3} (Σ = (3,1)), V = W ⊗ A, B = η ⊗ ω, G = O(3,1) × SL(2,ℝ). Then:

1. **(forcing)** B is the unique so(η)-equivariant bracket of family I up to scale, blind to signature [Lemma 1]; under the full Levi it is the only survivor.
2. **(no invariant lattice)** V carries no nonzero SO⁺(η)-invariant discrete subgroup; discreteness is possible only as a symmetry-broken state, and for finite states the effective point group is trivial [Lemma 2; Schur 📖].
3. **(lift)** Λ generates a lattice in H ⟺ Λ is β-integral; then H_Λ ∩ Z = exp(½c(Λ)ℤX); the type is a G-invariant. Solving MᵀBM = c·B₀ makes the β-integral lattices of a fixed type a single Sp(V, B)-orbit (stabiliser the arithmetic group of the type), so modulo G and scale the family has dimension dim Sp(2d) − dim G − 1 = d(2d+1) − (d(d−1)/2 + 3) − 1 = (3d²+3d)/2 − 4 (d = 4: 36 − 9 − 1 = 26) [Lemma 3].
4. **(compactifier)** every K̄_J is compact, and the adapted J = S_t ⊗ J_τ form one orbit G/K = H³ × ℍ² with K = SO(3) × SO(2) [Lemma 4ab; orbit-uniqueness not derived].
5. **(arithmeticity — the scope of finite volume)** for G-rational Λ, Γ_Λ is arithmetic and (H³×ℍ²)/Γ_Λ has finite volume; for split Λ = I_{3,1} ⊗ U, Γ_W = O(I_{3,1}) and O⁺(I_{3,1}) = [3,4,4] with covolume 0.0763304662; for β-integral but non-G-rational Λ, finite volume **does not occur** (infinite covolume, by Lemma 5(d)) [Lemma 5 + 📖 Borel–Harish-Chandra].
6. **(finite point groups = a pair (Λ, t))** for every J ∈ 𝒥 and every Λ, the point group K̄_J ∩ Γ̄_Λ is finite (compact ∩ discrete in Sp); for split Λ and adapted J with primitive timelike t, K̄_J ∩ Γ̄_Λ ≅ (O(L)_± × Aut(E_τ))/⟨(−1,−1)⟩ with L = Λ_W ∩ t^{⊥η} a positive rank-3 lattice, det L = |t·t|. The crystallographic class is the pair (Λ_W, t); the holohedry is O(L). The minimal section norm realising each holohedry is *exact*, n_min = (cubic 1, tetragonal 2, hexagonal 6, orthorhombic 7, monoclinic 11, trigonal 13, triclinic 43), by exhaustive search over det ≤ 43 [Lemma 6].
7. **(the section law — the heart)** for p ≢ 1 (mod 8): L is realised by a timelike section of I_{p,1} ⟺ disc L ≅ ℤ/n is cyclic with split bilinear form ⟨1/n⟩ (b_L(g,g) ≡ a/n, a a quadratic residue mod n); membership in the image is decided by the discriminant form (derived: the forward implication together with Lemma 7); the image itself — [3,7,13] at p = 2,3,4 and the mechanism at p = 5 — is measured. The law is stated through the discriminant *bilinear* form b_L (well-defined mod ℤ for any lattice, even or odd); the finer quadratic form q_L (mod 2ℤ) is not used, so the classification is parity-agnostic — the odd case runs through Lemma 7's parity argument, not Nikulin's even-lattice theorems. The forward implication is derived — w = v + t/n gives b_L(w,w) ≡ 1/n, checked on every section; the reverse — primitive embedding into the unimodular ambient — is *derived* for p ≢ 1 (mod 8) by gluing plus the odd-unimodular classification (Lemma 7), covering all measured signatures p = 2,3,4,5; at p ≡ 1 (mod 8) it **fails** — witness E₈ ⊕ ⟨2⟩ versus I₈ ⊕ ⟨2⟩ at p = 9, same discriminant form, different ambient parity. The image [3,7,13] is recovered from the discriminant form alone at p = 2,3,4 (13/13, by line-identical reproduction), and the mechanism holds at p = 5 (267/267); the rank-2 hexagonal gap is the quadratic-residue obstruction 2 ∉ (ℤ/3)^{×2}.
